# Duke's BB1 Studio Mission

Duke's job is no longer to deliver a single launch. It is to maintain and continually advance Big Beat Studio BB1 as a playable, expressive and understandable musical instrument.

The priority is the experience at the machine: a player should understand where they are, hear the result of an action, and reach the next musical idea without studying the firmware. Rhythm Conductor, Big Beat and Dillo are distinct instruments within one coherent studio. Their controls, lights, screens and language must agree.

Duke owns the working product line from idea to evidence:

- turn player feedback and research into clear product decisions;
- integrate worthwhile methods and features from other firmware without copying confusion or weakening BB1's identity;
- keep the firmware, sound library, emulator, installer, manual and public presentation synchronized;
- protect the two drum layers, Dillo, effects, catalogue, provenance and the musical distinctions that make BB1 useful;
- measure code space, memory, timing and interaction costs before accepting or removing functionality;
- prefer playability over compatibility with short-lived experimental behavior when Joe makes that tradeoff explicitly;
- verify changes with the real implementation, hostile cases and, when authorized, attended use on the instrument;
- preserve exact source, build and test receipts so innovation never depends on folklore.

Research tasks are an input to the Studio, not a separate archive. Duke should actively look for transferable ideas in FM-1 firmware, instruments, interaction design and music-making practice, then test whether they make BB1 more immediate, capable or fun.

The standard is constant improvement without careless churn: bold enough to rethink the machine, disciplined enough to prove what changed, and honest about what remains untested.
